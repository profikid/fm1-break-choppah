Your breaks

Put a pack of breaks of your own on the FM-1, up to sixteen, beside the eight built in. Load a sample, hear it chopped into sixteen slices exactly as CHOPPAH will play it, add it to the pack, and send the pack over USB. On the FM-1 the pack comes first, in its order: its first break is the one CHOPPAH starts with. Chrome or Edge, on a computer.

    A whole track: find its breaks

      The overlay: a longer loop under the breaks

      From the rest of the track (bass, chords, vocals: what the drums aren't), on the same bars as the breaks. CHOPPAH plays it under every break of the pack (CHOPPAH 1.7 and up), with its own fader.

      Under 95 BPM the tempo is doubled when a sample comes in: a two-bar loop at 175 is otherwise taken for one bar at 87.5. ×2 and ÷2 change it (the file then holds twice or half as many bars).

      Placing the slices: drag a slice's start (the amber lines) on the waveform; the close-up below shows the one you picked, 60 ms either side, where a click or a drag places it exactly. ← and → move it 1 ms (Shift: 10 ms), ↑ and ↓ pick the next or the previous one, Esc lets go. Each move plays the slice. The first slice starts at the loop's start and the last ends at its end. Changing the bars, tempo, slices or level starts the slices again; Reset slices does too.

      Warp to the grid tightens a loose loop: each slice's start is a warp marker, pulled onto its grid line (the dotted lines; every slice an eighth apart) and the sound between stretched or squeezed to fit, its pitch kept and each hit's attack untouched. Place the slices on the hits first, then warp; you keep moving them on the original, and hear and send the warped break.

      Click a slice, or press 1–8 and Q–I, to hear it alone. Chop plays the slices in a new order every bar, the way the generator rearranges a break. The preview repitches with the tempo, as CHOPPAH's SPEED mode.

      3. The pack

      Up to sixteen breaks, in the order they come on the FM-1. The flash holds 344 KB of them in two banks: at 22.05 kHz that is about ten breaks at 170 BPM (a break takes about 5,300 KB ÷ its tempo); a lower quality fits more.

        How this break loops on the FM-1 (with CHOPPAH 1.7 and up): kept in the pack, shared with it.

        CHOPPAH must be stopped: writing the flash pauses the sound. The pack replaces the breaks of your own already there. About half a minute for a full pack.

        The pack stays in this browser until you clear it. Export saves it as one file (.chpk: the breaks, their settings, the pack's name, theme and picture) to keep or share; Import reads one back (an older .chb too).

        4. The pack's name, look and hub

        A pack carries its name, who made it, a theme for the FM-1's screen and a picture for the pack hub. Each break keeps how it loops.

        Without a picture the hub draws one from the pack's name and theme.

        Uploads stay private on the hub until you publish them there.
